## 1. Mission and success condition

Build and document what a full Just One KISS marketing set looks like when every
piece shares the current website’s best visual language and the repository’s
controlling brand system:

> **Black first. Chrome second. Fire third. Mythic scale always. Ordinary never.**

self-aware but never campy, practical without becoming corporate, and unmistakably
Just One KISS without looking official, derivative, or generic.

1. A viewer recognizes every piece as part of the same system without requiring
   an identical layout.

   level of spectacle.

   rebuild the choices, verify the files, and explain every deviation.
5. The model can become the baseline for future variations without those
   variations silently rewriting it.
6. The end of every session leaves excellent accomplishment, evidence, decision,

## 5. Immutable baseline versus controlled variation

Until the owner explicitly requests a deviation, use **current-theme fidelity**.
The pristine model has three layers:

- Just One KISS is an independent theatrical rock tribute.
- No official affiliation, sponsorship, authorization, endorsement, or ownership
  is implied.
- Black dominates; chrome structures; fire-colored light concentrates energy;

## 6. The pristine creative grammar

### 6.1 Emotional and verbal behavior

Use **impact → image/proof → literal fact → honest action**.

- Headlines normally contain four to nine words.
- Write declarations with lighting-cue rhythm.
- Let one line rally the fan; do not make every line shout.
- Use arena, portal, ritual, night, shadow, fire, chrome, smoke, armor, road case,
  vault, signal, faithful, spectacle, entrance, chorus, full-scale, road-worn,
  towering, controlled, and electric only when they serve the idea.
- Avoid timid tribute apologies, generic “local band” language, unsupported
  superlatives, fake scarcity, official-sounding imitation, and legal filler in
  the hero.
- Keep safety, access, privacy, consent, contact, directions, and terms plain.

### 6.2 Visual hierarchy

Each composition should resolve in this order:

1. **Stop:** one monumental silhouette, performer, portal, artifact, or statement.
2. **Recognize:** Just One KISS identity and independent tribute context.
3. **Feel:** black depth, chrome edge, controlled red/orange heat, haze, and scale.
4. **Know:** mandatory facts in a highly legible live or controlled type layer.
5. **Act:** one CTA with a destination that fulfills it.
6. **Trust:** quiet independence, safety, access, or consent support where needed.

Preserve dark rest space. Do not fill every surface. Excess comes from scale,
material, silhouette, contrast, and attitude—not from uncontrolled clutter.

### 6.3 Palette roles

Use the controlled reference values, subject to placement-specific contrast tests:

| Role | Value | Use |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Stage black | `#050505` | dominant void and stage depth |
| Secondary black | `#0d0d10` | cards and backstage layers |
| Bone | `#f2f2ee` | critical text and hard highlights |
| Chrome | `#b8bcc2` | frames, armor, separators, proof |
| Blood red | `#b20d18` | concentrated danger and CTA heat |
| Fire orange | `#f06a21` | glow, action, transformation |
| Gold | `#d8a31a` | selective heritage/premium warmth |
| Signal yellow | `#f2c230` | verified facts and wayfinding |
| Cosmic purple | `#6d3fa9` | rare glam/night atmosphere |

Green may appear as a minor piece of authentic stage-light counterpoint; it is not
a core campaign color. Avoid pastel gradients and generic neon wallpaper.

### 6.4 Typography

- Use a cleared sharp, heavy, angular display face ceremonially for the identity,
  hero, section openings, badges, date hits, and short declarations.
- Use a heavy condensed poster sans for subheads and CTAs where suitable.
- Use a clean utility sans for body text, forms, directions, facts at small sizes,
  safety, accessibility, privacy, and disclaimers.
- Never set paragraphs or dense fact blocks in decorative type.
- Do not use the official KISS logo or reconstruct a protected wordmark.
- Treat `proto/public/assets/font/nasty.otf` and `script.otf` as implemented but
  uncleared until a font rights record proves the intended use.
- If font clearance is missing, preserve roles, rhythm, weight, and hierarchy with
  a cleared substitute; never falsely report visual identity as cleared.

### 6.5 Material and image language

Prefer original or cleared:

- Earl performance and portrait material;
- armor, bass, hardware, rivets, boots, truss, cables, control surfaces;
- road cases, pavilion/stage architecture, arrival-map motifs;
- hard beams, reflected light, restrained haze, black voids;
- portal, dossier, specimen, dispatch, poster-stack, and vault structures.

Generated atmosphere must not masquerade as documentary proof. Critical type is
added as a controlled layer. Reject malformed faces, anatomy, hands, tongue,
teeth, earrings, instruments, strings, frets, tuners, cables, armor, props,
reflections, shadows, or typography.

### 6.6 Earl fidelity and originality

When an owner-approved direction depicts Earl, preserve the inspected mature
facial structure, salt-and-pepper hair, long gray goatee, earrings, posture, and
performance energy. Do not silently turn Earl into Gene Simmons or a generic
young model.

Exact Gene/Demon-derived makeup, costume, likeness, marks, photos, and protected
design expression remain rights-sensitive. Where specific clearance is absent,
use an original tribute-safe angular mask and armor language rather than copying
the protected expression. Fidelity notes never equal rights clearance.

## 9. Canonical master-to-derivative rules

The canonical master is a system, not one flattened poster.

1. Keep expressive imagery separate from critical typography wherever the medium
   permits.
2. Keep the identity, mandatory facts, independence meaning, CTA meaning, and
   advisory meaning synchronized across derivatives.
3. Preserve one dominant center and intentional negative space in every ratio.
4. Recompose the performer, instrument, hands, headline, facts, and CTA for the

5. On narrow vertical formats, sequence information rather than shrinking it.
6. On wide formats, use a decisive subject/text split or centered portal; reduce

8. For print, verify bleed, trim, safe margin, color profile, resolution, QR/link,

9. For motion, use black/silhouette → chrome/fire discovery → complete fact/action

10. For audio, speak all mandatory facts and provide a transcript.

## 11. Rights, affiliation, safety, privacy, and accessibility

### 11.1 Rights and affiliation

- Always describe Just One KISS accurately as an independent theatrical rock
  tribute.
- Never use or simulate an official KISS logo, album/tour art, official recording,
  third-party photograph, exact protected design, or uncleared font/media.
- “In the repo,” “on the site,” “fan-submitted,” and “AI-generated” are not rights
  states.
- Record creator/source, people, releases, marks/design risk, edit rights,
  paid/organic scope, channel, territory, term, credit, revocation, checksum,
  reviewer, approval date, and expiry.

### 11.2 Effects and safety

Fire is palette, light, energy, or metaphor unless event-specific capability,
venue permission, safety plan, and wording are verified. The same rule applies to
sparks, fog, strobes/flashing patterns, platforms, blackout, projections, and
other show effects. Advisories are calm, proximate, literal, and never decorative.

### 11.3 Privacy and consent

Do not activate a form, pixel, tracking event, remarketing audience, media
submission, testimonial workflow, or update promise merely because a template or
route exists. Verify purpose, lawful/appropriate consent, storage, security,
retention/deletion, opt-out, moderation, response expectation, and reuse scope.

### 11.4 Accessibility

Preserve semantic text, logical reading order, keyboard focus, zoom/reflow,
contrast, descriptive actions, placement-specific alt text, captions/transcripts,
non-color cues, readable fact holds, reduced motion, and static equivalents.
Test the decorative face separately from the utility face. No critical fact may
exist only in an image, color, animation, audio, caption, hover state, or link.

## 14. Drift-control tests

Before accepting any piece into the pristine reference package, ask:

1. Could this advertise any local rock event? If yes, it is too generic.
2. Could it reasonably appear official or authorized by KISS, Gene Simmons, or a
   related rights holder? If yes, revise or hold.
3. Does black dominate while chrome structures and fire concentrates?
4. Is there one visual center, one proposition, and one action?
5. Does the display language recur ceremonially without harming utility clarity?
6. Does the piece feel tailored and excessive rather than cluttered or timid?
7. Are all four mandatory facts impossible to miss and exactly meaningful?
8. Does the CTA land on the promised content or function?
9. Is every dramatic claim supported and every sensitive unknown held?
10. Does Earl remain Earl when depicted, without becoming a copied celebrity or
    generic model?
11. Are accessibility, safety, consent, and independence calm and understandable?
12. Can another expert reproduce and audit the piece from its records?

Any failed answer triggers correction or `held`, never rationalization.
