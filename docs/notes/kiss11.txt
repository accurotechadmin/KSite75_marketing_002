3. Marketing Campaign Side: What I Understand
The first-run campaign is a five-piece funnel, not a random set of posters.

The campaign thesis is that the still images should behave like a month-by-month fan journey, with one unified theatrical world: black-dominant, chrome-framed, fire-lit, huge white display type, yellow fact chips, red/orange CTAs, smoke/fog atmosphere, road-case texture, stage truss silhouettes, and practical facts kept readable. 

The funnel is:

Awareness — audience learns the night exists.

Interest — audience understands why it feels different from a generic cover night.

Consideration — audience gets practical confidence: where, when, cost, camping, safety, arrival.

Conversion — audience joins updates / RSVPs without ticket-purchase language.

Retention & Nurture — audience shares, invites, asks questions, sends fan media, and returns for updates. 

Because this is a free show, the campaign must avoid “buy,” “tickets on sale,” “purchase,” “checkout,” VIP, paid admission, and scarcity language that implies a transaction. The approved conversion vocabulary is “RSVP appreciated,” “Join updates,” “Tell your crew,” “Plan your night,” and “No ticket required.”
