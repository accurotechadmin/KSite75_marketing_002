# Campaign Architecture

## Campaign definition

A campaign is a coordinated set of pieces that moves one or more audiences toward a defined behavior during a controlled window. It needs an objective, audience, proposition, family strategy, verified fact overlay, channel plan, destination, measurement plan, owners, budget status, and expiry.

## Planning stack

| Level | Required decision |
| --- | --- |
| Objective | What audience behavior should change? |
| Audience | Whose behavior, in what current state? |
| Funnel job | Awareness, interest, consideration, conversion, retention/nurture, or professional inquiry |
| Family | Which emotional/visual argument leads? |
| Proposition | What single promise will be remembered? |
| Proof | What approved evidence makes it credible? |
| Offer/facts | What exact volatile details apply? |
| Action | What one thing should happen next? |
| Destination | Can the route fulfill that action on mobile and without confusion? |
| Distribution | Organic, paid, owned, earned, partner, print, outdoor, direct |
| Measurement | What event indicates movement, and what quality guardrails apply? |
| Governance | Who owns facts, rights, safety, accessibility, release, and expiry? |

## Five-stage campaign spine

1. **Awareness — The night exists.** Optimize distinctive recognition, not detail density.
2. **Interest — The ritual is different.** Show sensory and craft proof.
3. **Consideration — Before you roll in.** Resolve practical barriers.
4. **Conversion without purchase — Get the drop.** Offer RSVP/updates/directions without paid-ticket language.
5. **Retention and nurture — Open the road case.** Reward attention, enable sharing, and continue community.

This spine derives from the existing first-run campaign. The nine families deepen it rather than replacing it.

## Piece architecture

Each piece record must answer:

- What does the audience currently believe?
- What one belief should change?
- Which family leads and why?
- What is the one message?
- What evidence appears?
- What exact facts appear and who verified them?
- What action and destination are promised?
- Which master composition and variants are required?
- Which disclaimer, advisory, consent, and accessibility treatments apply?
- How will success and harmful side effects be observed?
- When does it expire?

## Campaign naming

Use `JOK-[campaign]-[stage]-[family]-[piece]-[variant]-[revision]` with lowercase-safe slugs in filenames. Example: `JOK-2026EVENT-AWARE-ARENA-01-IG45-R01`. Do not put “final” in filenames; releases are identified by immutable release IDs.

## Sequencing patterns

- **Event attendance:** Arena Arrives → Shadow to Fire → One Night / Full Fire → Before the First Chord → Open the Road Case.
- **Fan devotion:** Love Letter with Volume → Chrome-Blood Spectacle → Join the Rally → Open the Road Case.
- **Professional:** Arena Arrives → Built to Land and Light → Love Letter with Volume → inquiry.
- **Utility correction:** One Night / Full Fire fact card → Before the First Chord detail → direct contact/FAQ.

## Landing-page congruence

The creative promise, CTA label, and first visible destination content must agree. A “Get directions” ad lands on directions, a “Watch” ad lands where video is immediately available, and “Open the vault” lands on actual content. Mismatch is a release blocker.
