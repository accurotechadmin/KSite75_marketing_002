# Campaign B — The Craft Behind the Heat

- **Audience lens:** `AUD-B` / Rational Evaluator
- **Core question:** Why should I believe the promise?
- **Current belief:** Theatrical language may hide imitation or unsupported hype.
- **Desired belief:** Original craft and disciplined choices make the promise credible.
- **Lead canon narrative:** The craft becomes visible.
- **Proposition:** Look closer: every convincing moment begins with intent.
- **Proof:** Cleared annotated detail—road-case hardware, lighting plot fragment, original costume material, or documented rehearsal process.
- **Concept:** A chrome specimen board with numbered callouts, proof labels, and an explicit “what this proves / what it does not prove” panel.
- **Visual grammar:** Modular grid, signal-yellow labels, hairline rules, mono data type, controlled red accents.
- **Voice:** Specific, candid, evidence-led.
- **Primary CTA:** `SEE THE PROOF`
- **Destination:** `site/index.html?lens=B#proof`
- **Primary event:** `open_proof_card`
- **Channel focus:** Reddit, YouTube explainers, Google demand placements, LinkedIn/editorial contexts, search-supporting landing content.

## Funnel sequence

1. Awareness: one surprising original detail.
2. Consideration: 5–8 card annotated craft carousel.
3. Interaction: open proof cards or ask a precise question.
4. Conversion: subscribe to sourced production notes—not generic hype.
5. WOM: request a structured, permissioned review.

## Proof card contract

Every card must include asset ID, observable detail, source/creator, rights status, claim supported, claim not supported, reviewer, and review date.

## Guardrails

Never manufacture statistics, testimonials, comparisons, credentials, or capability claims. A diagram is not evidence unless tied to a reviewed source object.
